\reviewer{Reviewer 1}
\begin{reviewcomment}
The adversary is assumed to reconstruct the transmit signals of individual
APs from its received observations. Since the received signal contains a
superposition of multiple AP transmissions, it is unclear how these
components can be reliably separated, especially in the presence of AN and
imperfect CSI.
\end{reviewcomment}
\begin{response}
We thank the reviewer for this important comment and agree that the
separation step requires an explicit treatment. Since all APs transmit common
symbols, the aggregate observation
$\mathbf{y}_a[n]=\sum_{k}\mathbf{H}_{a,k}\mathbf{x}_k[n]+\mathbf{n}_a[n]$
provides only $M_{\mathrm{UE}}$ observation dimensions per snapshot for
$N_{\mathrm{tx}}M_{\mathrm{tx}}$ per-AP transmit dimensions. Blind separation
of the per-AP components from $\mathbf{y}_a[n]$ alone is therefore not
possible in general, and we do not claim otherwise. The original manuscript
did not state clearly that this step is an assumption, and we have revised
the threat model accordingly.

Following the threat model of [12], the revised manuscript explicitly adopts
a \emph{genie-aided strong-adversary model}, in which the per-AP components
of the received samples are made available to the adversary. We adopt this
model deliberately, for the following reasons.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{Conservative privacy assessment.} The genie removes the
  inter-AP superposition, which is the main obstacle for a practical
  adversary seeking AP-dependent directions. The privacy of the proposed
  design is therefore assessed against an adversary that is granted a
  capability that a practical adversary does not have in general.
  \item \emph{Plausibility.} Partial per-AP separation may become available
  in practice, e.g., when AP-specific transmissions are resolvable in time
  or frequency, or when the propagation delays of the distributed APs are
  resolvable over a wide bandwidth.
  \item \emph{Design principle.} The network cannot verify the receiver
  capability of a served user. A privacy mechanism should therefore not
  rely on the adversary being unable to separate the per-AP signals.
\end{enumerate}

Regarding the AN, it is intentionally contained in the per-AP components
available to the adversary. Keeping the AN visible in the raw observations,
while nulling it at the legitimate combiner outputs, is the
receive-processing asymmetry on which the proposed design relies. Regarding
imperfect CSI, the adversary's channel-estimation error enters the residual
term $\widetilde{\mathbf{n}}_{a,k}^{\mathrm{adv}}[n]$ of the signal model.
Consistent with the strong-adversary model, the simulated adversary is
further given accurate knowledge of its per-AP channels $\mathbf{H}_{a,k}$,
which is now stated in Section~IV. The identifiability of the per-AP
reconstruction step is discussed in our response to Reviewer~2, Comment~2.
\end{response}

\begin{reviewcomment}
The sensing SNR expression in (2) needs further clarification. The
contributions from different APs are added without considering possible
coherent terms. In addition, the AN waveform is assumed to be known at the
legitimate sensing receivers, but its contribution is excluded from the
sensing SNR.
\end{reviewcomment}
\begin{response}
We thank the reviewer for this comment. We have revised the sensing model in
Section~II to state the receiver processing and the assumptions behind (2)
explicitly, and we address the two points below.

\textbf{Coherent cross terms.} Since all transmit APs radiate common symbols,
the echo received at receive AP $r$ is the superposition
$\sum_k\alpha_{r,k}\sqrt{g_{r,k}}\,\mathbf{a}^H(\phi_k)\mathbf{W}_k\mathbf{s}[n]$,
where $\mathbf{s}[n]$ collects the symbols and $\alpha_{r,k}$ is the complex
bistatic echo coefficient with $\mathbb{E}\{|\alpha_{r,k}|^2\}=\xi_{r,k}^2$.
Its instantaneous power therefore contains cross terms between transmit APs,
whose values depend on the relative phases of $\{\alpha_{r,k}\}_k$. These
phases are not known to the network, because the target position is known
only coarsely relative to the carrier wavelength ($\lambda_c\approx15.8$~cm
at $1.9$~GHz) and the target response varies with the aspect angle. We
therefore model them as independent and uniformly distributed across
transmit--receive AP pairs. Under this model, the cross terms vanish in
expectation, and (2) is the per-snapshot sensing SNR averaged over the echo
phases, which is why it is referred to as the \emph{average} sensing SNR.
The revised manuscript now states this assumption explicitly. A design that
exploits coherent superposition at the target would require phase-accurate
knowledge of the target position and phase synchronization of the transmit
APs at the target, which we do not assume for the sensing function.

\textbf{AN contribution.} The reviewer is correct that the AN echo was
excluded from (2) without explanation. Since $s_z[n]$ is known at the receive
APs and independent of the data and sensing symbols, its echo is separated by
matched filtering and could in principle be exploited. We keep it out of the
design objective deliberately. The AN echo from the true target is
proportional to $T_k=|\mathbf{a}^H(\phi_k)\mathbf{z}_k|^2$, so including it
in the objective would reward AN power in the true direction. This would
raise the true-direction peak of the beampattern reconstructed by the
adversary and work against the fake-peak constraint. Exploiting the AN echo
at the receivers after the design would add $\sum_kc_kT_k\ge0$ to
$\gamma_t$. Hence, the reported $\gamma_t$ is a lower bound on the achievable
sensing SNR, and the reported $3.6$~dB loss is an upper bound on the sensing
cost of the proposed design. Both points are now stated in Sections~II
and~III.
\end{response}

\begin{response}
We thank the reviewer for pointing out this inconsistency. The reviewer is

lower bound on the achievable sensing SNR, and the reported $3.6$~dB loss is

\begin{reviewcomment}
The entire threat model relies on the assumption that the adversarial user
can separate its received superposition into per-AP raw components and then
reconstruct $\hat{\mathbf x}_k[n]$ for each AP. However, the actual
observation is
$\mathbf y_a[n]=\sum_k\mathbf H{a,k}\mathbf x_k[n]+\mathbf n_a[n]$, while
each $\mathbf x_k[n]\in\mathbb C^{M_{\mathrm{tx}}}$ is unknown; with
$N_{\mathrm{tx}}M_{\mathrm{tx}}$ transmit dimensions but only
$M_{\mathrm{UE}}$ received dimensions per snapshot, this decomposition is
generally underdetermined. What identifiability conditions make the assumed
EM reconstruction of each AP waveform possible, particularly for the default
setting $N_{\mathrm{tx}}=8$, $M_{\mathrm{tx}}=16$, and $M_{\mathrm{UE}}=16$?
\end{reviewcomment}
\begin{response}
We thank the reviewer for this insightful comment and fully agree with the
dimension count. Since the APs transmit common symbols, $\mathbf{y}_a[n]$
offers $M_{\mathrm{UE}}=16$ observation dimensions per snapshot, against
$N_{\mathrm{tx}}M_{\mathrm{tx}}=128$ per-AP transmit dimensions in the
default setting. The decomposition of $\mathbf{y}_a[n]$ into per-AP
components is therefore underdetermined, and the original manuscript did not
state clearly that this step is an assumption rather than a consequence of
the signal model. We have revised the threat model to separate the attack
into two stages and to state the identifiability status of each.

\textbf{(i) Per-AP separation (assumed).} The separation of
$\mathbf{y}_a[n]$ into per-AP components is not identifiable from
$\mathbf{y}_a[n]$ alone in general. Following [12], we now state explicitly
that we adopt a genie-aided strong-adversary model, in which the per-AP
observations $\widetilde{\mathbf{y}}_{a,k}[n]$ are available to the
adversary. The genie removes the inter-AP superposition, which is the main
obstacle for a practical adversary, so the privacy of the proposed design is
assessed against an adversary that is granted a capability that a practical
adversary does not have in general. Since the network cannot verify the
separation capability of a served user, we consider it appropriate that the
defense does not rely on this capability being limited. Partial separation
may become available in practice, e.g., when AP-specific transmissions are
resolvable, or under wideband operation with resolvable inter-AP delays.

\textbf{(ii) Per-AP reconstruction (identifiable under a stated
condition).} Given
$\widetilde{\mathbf{y}}_{a,k}[n]=\widehat{\mathbf{H}}_{a,k}^{\mathrm{adv}}\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}_k[n]+\widetilde{\mathbf{n}}_{a,k}^{\mathrm{adv}}[n]$,
the recovery of $\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}_k[n]$ is a linear inverse problem
with $M_{\mathrm{UE}}$ equations and $M_{\mathrm{tx}}$ unknowns. It is
identifiable when $\widehat{\mathbf{H}}_{a,k}^{\mathrm{adv}}$ has full
column rank, which holds with probability one under Rayleigh fading whenever
$M_{\mathrm{UE}}\ge M_{\mathrm{tx}}$. The default setting satisfies this
condition with $M_{\mathrm{UE}}=M_{\mathrm{tx}}=16$. Moreover, since the
beampattern in (3) depends only on the covariance
$\mathbf{R}_{\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}_k}$, whose rank is at most
$N_{\mathrm{UE}}+1$ (the other users' streams, the sensing stream, and the AN
stream), a weaker sufficient condition is that
$\widehat{\mathbf{H}}_{a,k}^{\mathrm{adv}}$ is injective on the column space
of $[\mathbf{W}_{-a,k},\mathbf{z}_k]$. If $M_{\mathrm{UE}}<M_{\mathrm{tx}}$,
the adversary recovers only the projection of
$\mathbf{R}_{\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}_k}$ onto the row space of
$\widehat{\mathbf{H}}_{a,k}^{\mathrm{adv}}$, and the reconstructed
beampattern is distorted, which further favors the defender. The EM routine
of [12] jointly refines the channel and signal estimates within this per-AP
linear model. It does not resolve the inter-AP superposition, which is
removed by the genie.

We have also rewritten the sentence explaining why the attack operates on raw
samples, since in the original version it could be read as implying that raw
samples alone suffice for per-AP recovery. In addition, Section~IV now states
that the simulated adversary is given accurate knowledge of
$\mathbf{H}_{a,k}$, and that the reported success rates therefore
correspond to a conservative attack model.
\end{response}

\begin{reviewcomment}
Most of all, the legitimate sensing operation is not clearly described. For
example, although multiple RX APs are considered, the operation at each RX AP
(e.g., receive beamforming) and the combining between APs
(coherent/non-coherent, central/distributed) are not described. It is

\end{reviewcomment}
\begin{response}
We thank the reviewer for this comment and agree that the legitimate sensing
operation was not described in sufficient detail. The revised Section~II now
describes the processing chain as follows.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{Operation at each RX AP.} Each receive AP employs
  $M_{\mathrm{rx}}$ antennas and steers its receive beam toward the target.
  The resulting receive array gain is absorbed, together with the target
  RCS, in the scattering power $\xi_{r,k}^2$. Each receive AP then
  matched-filters its received samples with the known transmitted symbols.
  The data symbols are available at the central processor and therefore act
  as additional probing signals, while the echo of the known AN symbol is
  separated by matched filtering and discarded.
  \item \emph{Combining between RX APs.} The receive APs forward their
  matched-filter outputs to the central processor, which fuses them
  non-coherently. Non-coherent fusion avoids phase synchronization among the
  receive APs.
  \item \emph{How (2) is obtained.} At receive AP $r$, the echo is the
  superposition
  \begin{equation*}
  \sum_{k=1}^{N_{\mathrm{tx}}}\alpha_{r,k}\sqrt{g_{r,k}}\,\mathbf{a}^H(\phi_k)\mathbf{W}_k\mathbf{s}[n],
  \end{equation*}
  where $\mathbf{s}[n]$ collects the unit-power symbols and $\alpha_{r,k}$ is
  the bistatic echo coefficient with $\mathbb{E}\{|\alpha_{r,k}|^2\}=\xi_{r,k}^2$.
  Since the echo phases are not known to within the carrier wavelength, they
  are modeled as independent and uniformly distributed across
  transmit--receive AP pairs. Averaging over these phases and the symbols
  gives the per-snapshot SNR at receive AP $r$,
  \begin{equation*}
  \gamma_r=\frac{1}{\sigma_{\mathrm{rx}}^2}\sum_{k=1}^{N_{\mathrm{tx}}}\xi_{r,k}^2g_{r,k}\left\|\mathbf{a}^H(\phi_k)\mathbf{W}_k\right\|_F^2,
  \end{equation*}
  and (2) is its average $\gamma_t=\frac{1}{N_{\mathrm{rx}}}\sum_r\gamma_r$.
  \item \emph{Who experiences the SNR.} $\gamma_t$ is the average
  per-receive-AP SNR. The SNR of the fused statistic at the central
  processor is $N_{\mathrm{rx}}\gamma_t$. Since the two differ by a constant
  factor, they are maximized by the same beams, and the design is unaffected
  by this choice. All reported sensing SNRs are average per-receive-AP
  values, which is now stated in Section~IV.
\end{enumerate}
The detector applied to the fused statistic is described in our response to
Comment~2.
\end{response}

\begin{reviewcomment}
Furthermore, the authors have not described any detector or detection
threshold. Therefore, ``x dB sensing SINR loss'' doesn't represent anything.
How is it translated into the detection and false-alarm probabilities,
which eventually matter? Without this, it is very difficult to judge whether
the proposed scheme is practically useful.
\end{reviewcomment}
\begin{response}
We thank the reviewer for this important comment and agree that the SNR loss
should be related to detection performance. The revised manuscript now
specifies the detector and reports the corresponding detection
probabilities.

\textbf{Detector.} Each receive AP matched-filters its samples with the
$N_{\mathrm{s}}=N_{\mathrm{UE}}+1$ known data and sensing streams. With
unknown echo amplitudes and phases, the generalized likelihood ratio test
sums the matched-filter output energies over the $N_{\mathrm{s}}$ streams and
the $N_{\mathrm{rx}}$ receive APs, and compares the sum with a threshold
$\tau$. After normalization, the statistic follows a central $\chi^2$
distribution with $2N_{\mathrm{rx}}N_{\mathrm{s}}$ degrees of freedom under
the null hypothesis, so $\tau$ is set by
$\Pr\{\chi^2_{2N_{\mathrm{rx}}N_{\mathrm{s}}}>\tau\}=P_{\mathrm{fa}}$. Under
the target hypothesis, it follows a noncentral $\chi^2$ distribution with
noncentrality $2N\sum_r\gamma_r$, which gives
\begin{equation*}
P_{\mathrm{d}}=\mathbb{E}\Big\{Q_{N_{\mathrm{rx}}N_{\mathrm{s}}}\Big(\sqrt{2N{\textstyle\sum_r}\gamma_r},\sqrt{\tau}\Big)\Big\},
\end{equation*}
where $Q_{M}(\cdot,\cdot)$ is the generalized Marcum $Q$-function of order
$M$. Consistent with the random echo phases of the sensing model, the
per-receive-AP SNRs $\gamma_r$ are taken as independent and exponentially
distributed with mean $\gamma_t$, and the expectation is over this
fluctuation. Among fluctuation models with the same mean, this is a
conservative choice in the high-$P_{\mathrm{d}}$ regime.

\textbf{Detection performance.} For $P_{\mathrm{fa}}=10^{-6}$,
$N_{\mathrm{rx}}=4$, and $N_{\mathrm{s}}=5$, $P_{\mathrm{d}}=0.9$ requires
$\gamma_t\ge-8.5$~dB with integration over the $N=128$ snapshots. The
proposed design at $\eta\approx0.50$ ($\gamma_t=11.1$~dB) therefore retains
a margin of $19.6$~dB, compared with $23.2$~dB for the no-AN reference
($\gamma_t=14.7$~dB), and both designs achieve $P_{\mathrm{d}}>0.999$. Even
with the shortest integration that resolves all streams, $N=N_{\mathrm{s}}=5$,
the proposed design achieves $P_{\mathrm{d}}=0.998$, compared with $0.9999$
