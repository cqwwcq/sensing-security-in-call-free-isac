ADDITIONAL COMMENTS TO THE AUTHOR:
The paper proposes a fake-peak artificial noise design to protect sensing-location privacy in cell-free massive MIMO ISAC systems. The idea of exploiting the difference between post-combining detection and raw-sample reconstruction is interesting. However, several issues need to be addressed.
1. The adversary is assumed to reconstruct the transmit signals of individual APs from its received observations. Since the received signal contains a superposition of multiple AP transmissions, it is unclear how these components can be reliably separated, especially in the presence of AN and imperfect CSI.  
2. Constraint (9g) does not necessarily guarantee that the fake direction becomes the dominant peak of the reconstructed beampattern. Moreover, the condition used to derive (10) is not explicitly enforced in the optimization.  
3. The privacy evaluation considers only an adversary that estimates the target location from the dominant beampattern directions. It would be useful to examine whether the proposed method remains effective against more sophisticated localization strategies. Also, a two-dimensional visualization of the estimated target locations would help demonstrate whether the estimates are actually shifted toward the intended fake location.
4. The sensing SNR expression in (2) needs further clarification. The contributions from different APs are added without considering possible coherent terms. In addition, the AN waveform is assumed to be known at the legitimate sensing receivers, but its contribution is excluded from the sensing SNR.  
5. The simulation results could be strengthened. In particular, Fig. 4 compares the proposed robust design mainly with nominal schemes. Including a robust no-AN baseline would provide a fairer assessment of the benefit of the proposed approach.

ADDITIONAL COMMENTS TO THE AUTHOR:
The paper proposes a fake-peak artificial-noise design for protecting target-location information against a served user that reconstructs per-AP transmit beampatterns. The main idea is to exploit the difference between post-combining communication detection and raw multi-antenna observations, so that AN is nulled only at the legitimate combiner outputs while remaining visible to the reconstruction attack. The idea is interesting, however, the reviewer has the following concerns:

---- There is a potential inconsistency in the sensing-cost model. Equation (2) defines the sensing SNR using only the communication/sensing beam matrix $\mathbf {W_k} $ and excludes the AN beam $\mathbf {z_k} $, while the manuscript later states that $s_z[n]$ is known at the sensing receivers and that its echo can be separated from $s_t[n]$ by ideal matched filtering. If $s_z[n]$ is known, its target echo is itself a known probing signal and can, in principle, contribute additional sensing information; if it is deliberately discarded, the reported $3.6$ dB sensing-SNR loss is specific to that receiver definition. Can the sensing signal model explicitly state whether the AN echo is exploited, discarded, or treated as interference?

---- The entire threat model relies on the assumption that the adversarial user can separate its received superposition into per-AP raw components and then reconstruct $\hat{\mathbf x}_k[n]$ for each AP. However, the actual observation is $\mathbf y_a[n]=\sum_k\mathbf H{a,k}\mathbf x_k[n]+\mathbf n_a[n]$, while each $\mathbf x_k[n]\in\mathbb C^{M_{\mathrm{tx}}}$ is unknown; with $N_{\mathrm{tx}}M_{\mathrm{tx}}$ transmit dimensions but only $M_{\mathrm{UE}}$ received dimensions per snapshot, this decomposition is generally underdetermined. What identifiability conditions make the assumed EM reconstruction of each AP waveform possible, particularly for the default setting $N_{\mathrm{tx}}=8$, $M_{\mathrm{tx}}=16$, and $M_{\mathrm{UE}}=16$?

---- The literature review should be improved with recent works on multi-static ISAC:

R2---- X. Sun et al., "Performance Analysis and Optimization for Network-Assisted Full-Duplex Cell-Free Multi-Static ISAC Systems," in IEEE Transactions on Green Communications and Networking, vol. 10, pp. 272-288, 2026, doi: 10.1109/TGCN.2025.3575837.

---- Network-wide effective-channel nulling in (6) relies on coherent cancellation of the AN contributions transmitted by geographically distributed APs, i.e., $\sum_k\widehat{\mathbf g}_{i,k}^H\mathbf z_k=0$. This cancellation is sensitive not only to channel-estimation errors but also to residual inter-AP carrier-phase, timing, and frequency synchronization errors. Since the imperfect-CSI extension in (17)–(20) accounts only for isotropic channel-estimation errors, how robust is the central receive-processing asymmetry when the distributed AP phases are not perfectly synchronized?

---- The privacy evaluation assumes that the adversary always selects the dominant peak of each reconstructed beampattern and intersects those directions. An adversary aware of the fake-peak mechanism could instead retain multiple significant peaks, form all geometrically consistent multi-AP intersections, or explicitly test the hypothesis that one coherent set of peaks is artificial. What property of the proposed AN makes the true target location statistically unidentifiable under such a multi-hypothesis adversary? As currently formulated, the reported localization-success reduction appears to certify robustness against one specific reconstruction rule rather than sensing-location privacy in a broader sense.

ADDITIONAL COMMENTS TO THE AUTHOR:
This paper addresses concerns about the internal adversary, which the authors termed a reconstruction attack. The topic is timely, and the idea is interesting. However, there are several issues to be addressed.

1.  Most of all, the legitimate sensing operation is not clearly described. For example, although multiple RX APs are considered, the operation at each RX AP (e.g., receive beamforming) and the combining between APs (coherent/non-coherent, central/distributed) are not described. It is difficult to grasp how the sensing SINR in Eq.2 is obtained. Also, it is not clear who experiences the sensing SINR. Is it an individual RX AP, or the system as a whole?

2. Furthermore, the authors have not described any detector or detection threshold. Therefore, "x dB sensing SINR loss" doesn't represent anything. How is it translated into the detection and false-alarm probabilities, which eventually matter? Without this, it is very difficult to judge whether the proposed scheme is practically useful.

3. All comparisons are between different AN schemes. Considering that AN spends a significant portion of power, another baseline to compare with would be a no-AN alternative that allocates the corresponding power to communication while keeping comparable sensing performance. The authors should remember that it is an ISAC system. It is questionable whether treating communication merely as a minimum requirement while devoting more than half of the transmit power to creating a fake peak is a reasonable design choice for an ISAC system. The authors should elaborate further on the tradeoff between sensing, communication, and security.

4. In the experiment, the no-AN localization success rate is 36%, which is already low. What if the adversary is in a more favorable situation? Is AN still effective?

5. Minor comments.
- RCS is not specified in the experiments.
- Eq. 2 is not really an SINR. No interference there.
- TheRX-AP antenna number is missing.
