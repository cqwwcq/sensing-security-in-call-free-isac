\reviewer{Reviewer 1}
\begin{reviewcomment}
The adversary is assumed to reconstruct the transmit signals of individual
APs from its received observations. Since the received signal contains a
superposition of multiple AP transmissions, it is unclear how these
components can be reliably separated, especially in the presence of AN and
imperfect CSI.
\end{reviewcomment}
\begin{response}
We thank the reviewer for this important comment and agree that the
separation step requires an explicit treatment. Since all APs transmit common
symbols, the aggregate observation
$\mathbf{y}_a[n]=\sum_{k}\mathbf{H}_{a,k}\mathbf{x}_k[n]+\mathbf{n}_a[n]$
provides only $M_{\mathrm{UE}}$ observation dimensions per snapshot for
$N_{\mathrm{tx}}M_{\mathrm{tx}}$ per-AP transmit dimensions. Blind separation
of the per-AP components from $\mathbf{y}_a[n]$ alone is therefore not
possible in general, and we do not claim otherwise. The original manuscript
did not state clearly that this step is an assumption, and we have revised
the threat model accordingly.

Following the threat model of [12], the revised manuscript explicitly adopts
a \emph{genie-aided strong-adversary model}, in which the per-AP components
of the received samples are made available to the adversary. We adopt this
model deliberately, for the following reasons.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{Conservative privacy assessment.} The genie removes the
  inter-AP superposition, which is the main obstacle for a practical
  adversary seeking AP-dependent directions. The privacy of the proposed
  design is therefore assessed against an adversary that is granted a
  capability that a practical adversary does not have in general.
  \item \emph{Plausibility.} Partial per-AP separation may become available
  in practice, e.g., when AP-specific transmissions are resolvable in time
  or frequency, or when the propagation delays of the distributed APs are
  resolvable over a wide bandwidth.
  \item \emph{Design principle.} The network cannot verify the receiver
  capability of a served user. A privacy mechanism should therefore not
  rely on the adversary being unable to separate the per-AP signals.
\end{enumerate}

Regarding the AN, it is intentionally contained in the per-AP components
available to the adversary. Keeping the AN visible in the raw observations,
while nulling it at the legitimate combiner outputs, is the
receive-processing asymmetry on which the proposed design relies. Regarding
imperfect CSI, the adversary's channel-estimation error enters the residual
term $\widetilde{\mathbf{n}}_{a,k}^{\mathrm{adv}}[n]$ of the signal model.
Consistent with the strong-adversary model, the simulated adversary is
further given accurate knowledge of its per-AP channels $\mathbf{H}_{a,k}$,
which is now stated in Section~IV. The identifiability of the per-AP
reconstruction step is discussed in our response to Reviewer~2, Comment~2.
\end{response}

\begin{reviewcomment}
The entire threat model relies on the assumption that the adversarial user
can separate its received superposition into per-AP raw components and then
reconstruct $\hat{\mathbf x}_k[n]$ for each AP. However, the actual
observation is
$\mathbf y_a[n]=\sum_k\mathbf H{a,k}\mathbf x_k[n]+\mathbf n_a[n]$, while
each $\mathbf x_k[n]\in\mathbb C^{M_{\mathrm{tx}}}$ is unknown; with
$N_{\mathrm{tx}}M_{\mathrm{tx}}$ transmit dimensions but only
$M_{\mathrm{UE}}$ received dimensions per snapshot, this decomposition is
generally underdetermined. What identifiability conditions make the assumed
EM reconstruction of each AP waveform possible, particularly for the default
setting $N_{\mathrm{tx}}=8$, $M_{\mathrm{tx}}=16$, and $M_{\mathrm{UE}}=16$?
\end{reviewcomment}
\begin{response}
We thank the reviewer for this insightful comment and fully agree with the
dimension count. Since the APs transmit common symbols, $\mathbf{y}_a[n]$
offers $M_{\mathrm{UE}}=16$ observation dimensions per snapshot, against
$N_{\mathrm{tx}}M_{\mathrm{tx}}=128$ per-AP transmit dimensions in the
default setting. The decomposition of $\mathbf{y}_a[n]$ into per-AP
components is therefore underdetermined, and the original manuscript did not
state clearly that this step is an assumption rather than a consequence of
the signal model. We have revised the threat model to separate the attack
into two stages and to state the identifiability status of each.

\textbf{(i) Per-AP separation (assumed).} The separation of
$\mathbf{y}_a[n]$ into per-AP components is not identifiable from
$\mathbf{y}_a[n]$ alone in general. Following [12], we now state explicitly
that we adopt a genie-aided strong-adversary model, in which the per-AP
observations $\widetilde{\mathbf{y}}_{a,k}[n]$ are available to the
adversary. The genie removes the inter-AP superposition, which is the main
obstacle for a practical adversary, so the privacy of the proposed design is
assessed against an adversary that is granted a capability that a practical
adversary does not have in general. Since the network cannot verify the
separation capability of a served user, we consider it appropriate that the
defense does not rely on this capability being limited. Partial separation
may become available in practice, e.g., when AP-specific transmissions are
resolvable, or under wideband operation with resolvable inter-AP delays.

\textbf{(ii) Per-AP reconstruction (identifiable under a stated
condition).} Given
$\widetilde{\mathbf{y}}_{a,k}[n]=\widehat{\mathbf{H}}_{a,k}^{\mathrm{adv}}\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}_k[n]+\widetilde{\mathbf{n}}_{a,k}^{\mathrm{adv}}[n]$,
the recovery of $\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}_k[n]$ is a linear inverse problem
with $M_{\mathrm{UE}}$ equations and $M_{\mathrm{tx}}$ unknowns. It is
identifiable when $\widehat{\mathbf{H}}_{a,k}^{\mathrm{adv}}$ has full
column rank, which holds with probability one under Rayleigh fading whenever
$M_{\mathrm{UE}}\ge M_{\mathrm{tx}}$. The default setting satisfies this
condition with $M_{\mathrm{UE}}=M_{\mathrm{tx}}=16$. Moreover, since the
beampattern in (3) depends only on the covariance
$\mathbf{R}_{\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}_k}$, whose rank is at most
$N_{\mathrm{UE}}+1$ (the other users' streams, the sensing stream, and the AN
stream), a weaker sufficient condition is that
$\widehat{\mathbf{H}}_{a,k}^{\mathrm{adv}}$ is injective on the column space
of $[\mathbf{W}_{-a,k},\mathbf{z}_k]$. If $M_{\mathrm{UE}}<M_{\mathrm{tx}}$,
the adversary recovers only the projection of
$\mathbf{R}_{\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}_k}$ onto the row space of
$\widehat{\mathbf{H}}_{a,k}^{\mathrm{adv}}$, and the reconstructed
beampattern is distorted, which further favors the defender. The EM routine
of [12] jointly refines the channel and signal estimates within this per-AP
linear model. It does not resolve the inter-AP superposition, which is
removed by the genie.

We have also rewritten the sentence explaining why the attack operates on raw
samples, since in the original version it could be read as implying that raw
samples alone suffice for per-AP recovery. In addition, Section~IV now states
that the simulated adversary is given accurate knowledge of
$\mathbf{H}_{a,k}$, and that the reported success rates therefore
correspond to a conservative attack model.
\end{response}

\textit{Section II:} ``One served user, indexed by $a$, is treated as an
internal adversary. It knows its own pilots, data symbols, combiner
$\mathbf{u}_a$, and the transmit-AP positions, and can form its own estimate
$\widehat{\mathbf{H}}_{a,k}^{\mathrm{adv}}$ of its channel
$\mathbf{H}_{a,k}$. Since all APs transmit common symbols, $\mathbf{y}_a[n]$
offers only $M_{\mathrm{UE}}$ dimensions for $N_{\mathrm{tx}}M_{\mathrm{tx}}$
per-AP transmit dimensions, so its per-AP decomposition is not identifiable
from $\mathbf{y}_a[n]$ alone in general. Following [12], we therefore adopt a
genie-aided strong-adversary model in which the per-AP components of the
received samples are available to the adversary, as may occur when
AP-specific transmissions are resolvable in time, frequency, or delay. Since
the network cannot verify the receiver capability of a served user, this
conservative model avoids relying on the adversary being unable to separate
them. The attack operates on raw multi-antenna samples, since applying
$\mathbf{u}_a$ would collapse each snapshot into a scalar. After decoding its
own data symbol $s_a[n]$, the adversary subtracts its data component using
its channel estimate $\widehat{\mathbf{H}}_{a,k}^{\mathrm{adv}}$, which
leaves
$\widetilde{\mathbf{y}}_{a,k}[n]=\widehat{\mathbf{H}}_{a,k}^{\mathrm{adv}}\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}_k[n]+\widetilde{\mathbf{n}}_{a,k}^{\mathrm{adv}}[n]$
with the non-self transmit component
$\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}_k[n]=\sum_{j\neq a}\mathbf{w}_{j,k}s_j[n]+\mathbf{w}_{t,k}s_t[n]$,
and $\widetilde{\mathbf{n}}_{a,k}^{\mathrm{adv}}[n]$ collecting the receiver
noise and the residual caused by adversary-side channel-estimation errors.
It then applies an expectation-maximization routine to obtain
$\widehat{\mathbf{x}}_k[n]\approx\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}_k[n]$, which is
identifiable when $\widehat{\mathbf{H}}_{a,k}^{\mathrm{adv}}$ has full
column rank, i.e., with probability one for $M_{\mathrm{UE}}\ge
M_{\mathrm{tx}}$, and forms the AP-dependent beampattern from the sample
covariance over the available snapshots.''
\end{revisedtext}
